# Binance

Binance is a cryptocurrency exchange founded in 2017. Its founders include
Changpeng Zhao and Yi He. Its history includes expansion into related services, US
criminal and regulatory proceedings, and major security incidents.
As of 31 October 2026, the company was investigating a newly disclosed
hot-wallet theft that it valued at approximately $6.3 billion.[^about][^doj][^incident]

## Company and related platforms

Binance dates its launch to July 2017. Its company profile describes a
broader group of activities including exchange services, research,
education, and charity. As of October 2026, it listed Richard Teng and
Yi He as co-CEOs, and Zhao as co-founder and former CEO.[^about]

Binance.US launched in September 2019. Its own documentation identifies
BAM Trading Services as its operator and says its matching engine and
wallet technologies are licensed from Binance. Statements about the global
exchange, the US platform, and individual group companies therefore need
to identify the entity concerned.[^us]

## Regulatory and criminal proceedings

In June 2021, the UK's Financial Conduct Authority imposed restrictions
on Binance Markets Limited. Its notice said that this company could not
undertake regulated activities without prior written consent. The FCA
said in August 2021 that the company had complied with the requirements,
while the restrictions remained in place. A later update records the
cancellation of Binance Markets Limited's FCA permissions on 30 May 2023.
The notices concern the regulatory position of named entities; they do not
amount to a ban on every cryptocurrency transaction by UK residents.[^fca]

On 21 November 2023, Binance Holdings Limited pleaded guilty to offences
involving the Bank Secrecy Act, money-transmitter registration, and the
International Emergency Economic Powers Act. The Department of Justice
announced $4.3 billion in penalties, compliance remediation, and an
independent monitor. Zhao separately pleaded guilty to failing to maintain
an effective anti-money-laundering programme.[^doj]

In May 2025, the SEC announced a joint stipulation dismissing its separate
civil action against Binance entities and Zhao with prejudice, describing
the decision as a discretionary policy matter. In October 2025, President
Donald Trump pardoned Zhao; the clemency warrant is dated 21 October.
These were separate developments concerning the civil case and Zhao's
criminal case respectively.[^sec][^pardon]

## Security incidents

### 2019 hot-wallet theft

On 7 May 2019, Binance disclosed a withdrawal of 7,000 BTC by attackers.
It said API keys and two-factor authentication codes had been compromised,
with other information potentially affected. It suspended deposits and
withdrawals for a security review and committed its Secure Asset Fund for
Users, or SAFU, to cover the loss.[^2019]

### 2022 BNB Chain bridge exploit

BNB Chain reported an exploit of BSC Token Hub, the [bridge](blockchain-bridge.md)
between BNB Beacon Chain and BNB Smart Chain, involving 2 million BNB.
Its account attributed the exploit to a forged proof and described
coordinating with validators to halt the chain. This involved the bridge
infrastructure and should be distinguished from a theft from exchange
customer accounts.[^bridge]

### October 2026 theft

Binance's 30 October update said hot wallets had been compromised at
approximately 20:05 UTC on 29 October, with about $6.3 billion in tokens
stolen. It said roughly $3 billion had been frozen onchain and more than
$6.34 billion in company funds had been set aside to cover losses, with
some replacement tokens still being purchased. These were company-reported
figures and commitments, not confirmation that all recovery work was
complete.[^incident]

The same update said the investigation had expanded beyond an initially
suspected compromised laptop and had found no evidence of leaked wallet
private keys. Withdrawals remained suspended in that update, while
deposits and unaffected services were operating. As of this article's
31 October update, the cited technical account remained preliminary.[^incident]
