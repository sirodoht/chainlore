# Stablecoins Ordinance (Hong Kong)

The **Stablecoins Ordinance** (Cap. 656) is a Hong Kong law that makes the
issuing of fiat-referenced [stablecoins](stablecoin.md) a licensed activity
supervised by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). It was passed by the
Legislative Council on 21 May 2025 and came into operation on 1 August
2025.[^gia-commence][^pr-2025-05] It applies to issuers in Hong Kong and
also to anyone who issues a coin outside Hong Kong that purports to be
stable against the Hong Kong dollar. It limits who may offer such coins to
the public, sets minimum capital, reserve and redemption rules, and bars
licensees from paying interest on their coins.[^ord] The HKMA granted the
first two licences on 10 April 2026, to Anchorpoint Financial and
HSBC.[^pr-0410] As of October 2026 both issuers had begun phased rollouts
of Hong Kong dollar coins.[^block-hkdap][^decrypt-redcoin]

## Background

The HKMA published a discussion paper on crypto-assets and stablecoins in
January 2022 and a summary of the feedback in January 2023, which proposed
bringing certain stablecoin activities within the regulatory
perimeter.[^hkma-page] In December 2023 the Financial Services and the
Treasury Bureau (FSTB) and the HKMA issued a consultation paper proposing a
licensing regime for issuers of fiat-referenced stablecoins. The paper
proposed that only licensed issuers and specified licensed entities could
offer such coins, that only coins licensed by the Monetary Authority could
be offered to retail investors, and that unlicensed issuance could not be
advertised. The consultation ran until 29 February
2024.[^pr-2023-12][^hkma-page]

On 12 March 2024 the HKMA launched a stablecoin issuer sandbox, to tell
interested firms what supervisors expected and to collect feedback on the
proposals.[^pr-2024-03] On 18 July 2024 it named the first participants:
JINGDONG Coinlink Technology Hong Kong, RD InnoTech, and a group made up of
Standard Chartered Bank (Hong Kong), Animoca Brands and Hong Kong
Telecommunications (HKT). The HKMA said participants would not handle
public funds at the initial stage.[^pr-2024-07-sandbox]

The consultation conclusions followed on 17 July 2024. The FSTB and the
HKMA reported 108 submissions, and said a vast majority of respondents
agreed a regime should be introduced.[^pr-2024-07-concl]

## Legislative history

The Stablecoins Bill was submitted to the Legislative Council for first
reading on 18 December 2024.[^block-bill] The Legislative Council passed it
on 21 May 2025, and the Government said the Ordinance was expected to take
effect that year to give the industry time to understand the licensing
requirements.[^pr-2025-05] The Ordinance was gazetted on 30 May 2025. On 6
June 2025 the Government gazetted a notice appointing 1 August 2025 as the
commencement date, as section 1(2) of the Ordinance left the date to the
Secretary for Financial Services and the Treasury.[^gia-commence]

## Provisions

### What a stablecoin is

The Ordinance defines a stablecoin as a cryptographically secured digital
representation of value that is used or intended to be used as a medium of
exchange accepted by the public (for payment, discharging a debt or
investment), operates on a distributed ledger, and purports to keep a
stable value against a single asset or a basket of assets. It excludes,
among other things, tokens issued by central banks or governments,
securities and futures contracts, stored value facility deposits, and bank
deposits.[^ord]

A "specified stablecoin" is one that purports to keep a stable value wholly
against official currencies, or against units of account or stores of value
that the Monetary Authority specifies by gazetted notice, or a combination
of these. The Monetary Authority can also specify further classes of
digital representation of value.[^ord] The HKMA describes the resulting
regime as covering the issuance of fiat-referenced stablecoins.[^hkma-page]

### Who needs a licence

A person carries on a regulated stablecoin activity if they issue a
specified stablecoin in Hong Kong in the course of business. The same
applies to a person who issues a specified stablecoin outside Hong Kong
that purports to keep a stable value, wholly or partly, against the Hong
Kong dollar. The Monetary Authority may, after consulting the Financial
Secretary, specify further activities by gazetted notice.[^ord] Section 5
of the Ordinance also treats a person as holding out as carrying on a
regulated activity if they actively market it to the public, in Hong Kong
or elsewhere.[^ord]

Applicants must be a company or an authorised institution incorporated
outside Hong Kong. The Monetary Authority may grant a licence only if
satisfied that the minimum criteria in Schedule 2 would be met, and may
attach conditions.[^ord]

### Who may offer stablecoins

Section 9 makes it an offence to offer a specified stablecoin, or hold
oneself out as offering one, unless an exemption applies. The main
exemption covers a "permitted offeror" where the issue of the coin is
authorised by a licence. Permitted offerors are licensees, virtual asset
service licensees, stored value facility licensees, corporations licensed
for Type 1 (dealing in securities) regulated activity, and authorised
institutions.[^ord] A second exemption allows permitted offerors to offer
coins that are not licensed to a class of persons specified by the
Financial Secretary. On 6 June 2025 the Financial Secretary specified
professional investors for this purpose.[^ord][^gia-commence] The
Government stated that only coins issued by a licensed issuer may be
offered to retail investors.[^pr-2025-05]

Advertising is also restricted. Publishing an advertisement in which an
unlicensed person holds themselves out as carrying on a regulated activity
is an offence, as is advertising the offering of a coin that does not fall
under the exemptions above.[^ord] The Government said that, to prevent
fraud, only advertisements for licensed issuance are allowed at all times,
including during the transitional period.[^pr-2025-05]

### Minimum criteria for licensees

Schedule 2 of the Ordinance sets the minimum criteria that licensees must
meet.[^ord]

- Capital. A licensee that is not an authorised institution must have
  adequate financial resources and liquid assets, and paid-up share capital
  of at least HK$25 million or other approved financial resources of an
  equivalent amount.
- Reserves. For each type of coin, the licensee must hold a segregated pool
  of reserve assets whose market value is at all times at least equal to
  the par value of coins in circulation. The pool must generally be held in
  the same reference asset as the coin, must be protected against the
  licensee's other creditors, and must consist of high quality, highly
  liquid assets with minimal investment risk. Reserves must be subject to
  regular independent attestation and audit.
- Redemption. Holders must have a right to redeem. Unless the Monetary
  Authority consents otherwise, a valid request must be honoured as soon as
  practicable at par value in the reference asset, less any reasonable fee,
  and redemption conditions must not be unduly burdensome. If the licensee
  becomes insolvent, holders have a right to direct disposal of the reserve
  pool to redeem coins pro rata, and a claim against the licensee for any
  shortfall.
- Disclosure. Licensees must disclose their reserve management policy, risk
  assessments, and the composition, market value and audit or attestation
  results of reserves, and must set out redemption rights, fees and
  processing times. They must publish a white paper for each type of coin
  and give holders access to complaint handling and redress.
- Interest. A licensee must not pay, or permit anyone to pay, interest on
  its coins, defined as any return represented to arise from holding the
  coin by reference to the holding period, par value or market value.
- Other. Licensees must apply anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist
  financing controls, meet fit-and-proper requirements for their chief
  executive, directors and controllers, maintain risk management and
  recovery and orderly wind-down plans, and obtain the Monetary Authority's
  consent before carrying on other business activities.

### Penalties

Carrying on a regulated stablecoin activity without a licence, or offering
a specified stablecoin outside the exemptions, is punishable on summary
conviction by a fine of HK$500,000 and two years' imprisonment, with a
further daily fine for a continuing offence. On conviction on indictment
the penalty is a fine of HK$5 million and seven years' imprisonment.[^ord]
Fraud or deception in a transaction involving a specified stablecoin
carries up to HK$10 million and ten years' imprisonment on indictment, and
the court may bar the offender from dealing in stablecoins in Hong Kong for
up to five years.[^ord] Unlawful advertising carries a fine at level 5 and
six months' imprisonment.[^ord]

### Transitional arrangements

Schedule 7 allowed firms already carrying on a regulated activity in Hong
Kong before 1 August 2025 to continue for the first three months. If such a
firm applied within those three months, the Monetary Authority acknowledged
the application and the firm gave a declaration and an undertaking to
comply as if licensed, it could continue for the first six months. The
Monetary Authority could grant a provisional licence within that period if
it was satisfied the firm had a reasonable prospect of showing it could
meet the requirements, or give a rejection notice that the firm could
object to.[^ord] The HKMA published an explanatory note on the transitional
provisions for pre-existing issuers on 29 July 2025.[^pr-2025-07] The HKMA
stated that, on that date, no licence had been issued.[^pr-2025-07]

### HKMA guidelines

On 29 July 2025 the HKMA published finalised guidelines on the supervision
of licensed stablecoin issuers and on anti-money laundering and
counter-financing of terrorism for licensed stablecoin issuers, together
with consultation conclusions on each and an explanatory note on licensing.
The guidelines were published in the Gazette on 1 August 2025.[^pr-2025-07]
The HKMA says they set out its regulatory expectations for licensed
issuers.[^hkma-page] The HKMA asked interested applicants to contact it by
31 August 2025 and said those wishing to be considered early should apply
by 30 September 2025.[^pr-2025-07]

## Implementation

### Licensing

The HKMA said it received applications from 36 entities in the first batch,
by the 30 September 2025 deadline. It granted licences on 10 April 2026 to
Anchorpoint Financial Limited and The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking
Corporation Limited, saying it had focused on applicants' risk management
capability and on whether they could propose distinct use cases with viable
business plans.[^insight-2026] The licences took effect that day.[^pr-0410]
Anchorpoint is a joint venture of Standard Chartered Bank (Hong Kong), HKT
and Animoca Brands.[^charltons] The register of licensed issuers lists them
as licences FRS01 and FRS02; the version this article read was last revised
on 23 April 2026.[^register]

The HKMA said in April 2026 that it would continue to engage the remaining
applicants and that its stance on further licences was "open yet prudent",
with no definitive inclination at that stage.[^insight-2026] This article
found no report of a further licence granted by 31 October 2026.

Earlier, in July 2025, Eddie Yue, the HKMA's chief executive, said the
market needed to "further rein in the euphoria" and that few licences would
be granted initially, so most applicants would be disappointed.[^ledger] In
August 2025 he said the HKMA had been in preliminary communication with
dozens of parties, and that only a handful of licences would be granted
initially.[^pr-2025-08]

### Launches

Anchorpoint began a phased rollout of HKDAP, a Hong Kong dollar stablecoin,
on 12 August 2026. The Block reported that access was through beta access
for institutional distributors and professional investors, and that HashKey
Exchange completed the first minting and redemption.[^block-hkdap] This
article did not find a retail launch date for HKDAP.

HSBC said when it received its licence that it planned to launch a Hong
Kong dollar stablecoin in the second half of 2026, through its PayMe and
HSBC HK mobile banking apps, for peer-to-peer and merchant payments, with
tokenised investment subscriptions through the app.[^hsbc-pr] On 28 April
2026 the HKMA warned of tokens named "HKDAP" and "HSBC" circulating in the
market with no connection to the licensees, and HSBC denied any
involvement.[^block-fake] On 30 September 2026 HSBC named its coin
RedCoin.[^investinglive-name] Decrypt reported that RedCoin (RCHKD) was
activated inside PayMe on Friday 23 October 2026, with more than 3.3
million PayMe users able to activate wallets in the following days. The
report said it ran on HSBC's own private ledger, was not a bank deposit and
was not covered by the Deposit Protection Scheme. HSBC's reserve tracker
showed at the time about US$10.2 million in circulation against US$10.4
million of cash reserves, and HSBC expected the first independent reserve
attestation after the fourth quarter of 2026.[^decrypt-redcoin] The Paypers
dated its report 27 October 2026 and said access was phased.[^paypers] As
of October 2026, an analyst note on investinglive.com said the coin could
only be used inside HSBC's own apps.[^investinglive-launch]

## Mainland China and offshore yuan coins

Mainland authorities have reportedly shaped who applies. In October 2025
the Financial Times reported, citing unnamed sources, that the People's
Bank of China and the Cyberspace Administration of China had told Ant Group
and JD.com not to go ahead with plans to issue yuan-pegged stablecoins in
Hong Kong. The Block's summary reported that five sources said officials
objected to private companies issuing any form of currency, and that one
said such coins could challenge the digital yuan. The Block reported that
neither firm nor the regulators had publicly confirmed the instruction in
the pieces it summarised.[^block-ft][^decrypt-beijing] Decrypt reported
that Ant Group and JD.com had not responded to its request for comment, and
that the regulators' guidance was reported only through the
FT.[^decrypt-beijing] The Block, citing the FT, also reported that the HKMA
had said in September 2025 that 77 firms, including Ant and JD.com, had
expressed interest in a licence.[^block-ft] JD's Hong Kong unit, JINGDONG
Coinlink, had been a sandbox participant in 2024.[^pr-2024-07-sandbox]

On 3 October 2025, after reports that a Hong Kong firm, AnchorX, had issued
the first offshore yuan stablecoin, the HKMA stated that no licences had
been granted to any stablecoin issuer and urged the public to be cautious
about such projects.[^coincentral] The Ordinance covers any specified
stablecoin issued in Hong Kong in the course of business, which would
include one referencing the yuan.[^ord] As of 31 October 2026 this article
found no HKMA licence for a yuan-referenced coin, as the register this
article read listed only the two April 2026 licensees.[^register]

## Criticism and warnings

### Speculation

On 14 August 2025 the HKMA and the Securities and Futures Commission (SFC)
issued a joint statement on abrupt share price movements linked to the
stablecoin concept after announcements, news reports and social media posts
about plans to apply for licences. They urged the public not to make
investment decisions on market hype, reminded market participants not to
make misleading statements, and said the SFC would act against
manipulation. Yue said the HKMA had a "rigorous and prudent approach" and
that approval thresholds were high.[^pr-2025-08] In July 2025, Yue had
warned of a risk of bubbles, observing that some listed companies' shares
jumped after merely announcing stablecoin plans.[^standard-yue]

The HKMA warned the public against scams from the start. When the sandbox
participants were named in July 2024 it advised vigilance against scams
purporting to be related to the sandbox, and in April 2026 it warned that
fake tokens were using the licensees'
names.[^pr-2024-07-sandbox][^block-fake]

### Narrow licensing and limited use

The HKMA has said it would grant only a handful of licences at
first.[^pr-2025-08] An analyst note on RedCoin's launch said the coin
risked being a closed system that adds little to existing PayMe
balances.[^investinglive-launch]
