Dogecoin (ticker DOGE) is a cryptocurrency. According to the project’s history page, it was originally created as a joke in late 2013 by Billy Markus and Jackson Palmer, and its launch took influence from the Doge meme.[^1] The Dogecoin Foundation describes itself as a not-for-profit organisation established in 2014 to support the currency through development and advocacy.[^2] In February 2021, CNBC reported that Dogecoin rallied 65% in 24 hours to a record high after Elon Musk and a number of celebrities appeared to back it on Twitter.[^5]

## History

According to the project’s history page, Dogecoin was originally created as a joke in late 2013 by Billy Markus and Jackson Palmer, who both thought cryptocurrency was being taken far too seriously. Palmer created the Dogecoin.com website and would have been, for a while, its public face, while Markus worked as a solo developer on the first four releases.[^1]

The page says Dogecoin launched on 6 December 2013, taking influence from the Doge meme, an image of a Shiba Inu dog with multi-coloured comic sans text. It says the meme was based on a 2010 photograph of a Japanese female Shiba Inu called Kabosu.[^1]

The page says Dogecoin was a near-instant success and gained popularity on sites such as Reddit, where it was, and still is, used as a tipping currency. It also reports that two weeks after launch Dogecoin had more daily transactions than Bitcoin, and that its website had over one million unique visitors in the first month. These are the project’s own claims and have not been independently verified.[^1]

The page says that in 2014 both founders left the development efforts while a Dogecoin Core Development team was formed. It describes the team as a small group of core maintainers, supported by more than 40 contributors over the years.[^1]

The page also describes charitable efforts. It says the community raised 26.5 million DOGE, worth around USD 30,000 at the time, in 2014 to send the Jamaican bobsled team to the Sochi Winter Olympics, and that in March of that year it raised over 40 million DOGE to help build clean-water wells in Kenya. It says that most recently the Foundation partnered with YouTubers Mr Beast and Mark Rober on the TeamSeas initiative, which aimed to raise a combined 30 million U.S. dollars to remove 30 million pounds of trash from the world’s oceans.[^1]

CNBC reported that Dogecoin rallied 65% in 24 hours to a record high of $0.083745 at 5:30 p.m. ET on a Sunday, according to CoinMarketCap data, after billionaire Elon Musk and a number of celebrities appeared to back the cryptocurrency on Twitter. It said the token’s market value briefly hit a high of $10.7 billion that Sunday.[^5] CNBC said retail investors had pumped up the price, “taking their cue from the tweets of Tesla CEO Elon Musk”, and that the resurgence had also been down to enthusiasm from a Reddit group called SatoshiStreetBets.[^5]

Coinpaper, in a report dated 10 March 2026, says Musk confirmed the timeline for X Money, a payments service.[^imp-25-3] A Cryptorank copy of that report describes X Money as the payments platform developed by Elon Musk’s social media company X.[^imp-25-1] According to the report, X Money is set to enter early public access in April 2026, and it describes features including direct deposits, yield on balances and peer-to-peer payments.[^imp-25-3] The Paypers, a payments trade outlet, separately reports that officials of X confirmed the April 2026 early-access plan.[^imp-25-2] The report says Dogecoin integration remains unconfirmed and that X has yet to announce any concrete cryptocurrency integration.[^imp-25-3] The same report says the DOGE price rose more than 8% in a single day around the time of Musk’s announcement, which it attributes to market speculation rather than any confirmed development.[^imp-25-3]

## Technology

A CoinDesk page description, which is the only substantive text retrieved from that article, says Litecoin, described there as a blockchain cloned from Bitcoin in 2011, provides network security to Dogecoin through a process called merged mining.[^4]

The opened sources do not describe Dogecoin’s proof-of-work design, its Scrypt mining parameters, its block timing, or the activation date of merged mining, so those details are not covered here.[^1][^4]

## Governance

The Foundation’s About page says the Dogecoin Foundation is a not-for-profit organisation established to provide support for Dogecoin through development and advocacy, Dogecoin trademark defence to prevent abuse and fraud, and a trailmap for the future of Dogecoin. It says the Foundation was established by members of the Dogecoin team in 2014, and that in 2021 it was re-invigorated by members of the original core team along with new seasoned advisors.[^2]

The same page says the Foundation operates as Much Wow Ltd and MadeUpNumbers Ltd, registered companies in England and Wales, and that it operates in the United States as Dogecoin Foundation and Much Wow Inc, and in Australia as Dogecoin Foundation Australia. The research did not check company registries, so these entity details rest on the Foundation’s own description.[^2]

## Incidents and controversies

CNBC reported that Musk said his tweets about dogecoin are meant to be taken as jokes. It quoted him on the social audio app Clubhouse saying that “the most entertaining outcome and the most ironic outcome would be that dogecoin becomes the currency of Earth in the future.”[^5] The same article notes that Musk has tweeted about dogecoin several times over the years.[^5]

Coinpaper reports that X has not issued any official statement confirming cryptocurrency functionality. It says Musk re-posted a third-party forecast projecting future features, including crypto integration, and that this was not an official company announcement.[^3]

The research identified no primary source or independent reporting of wrongdoing by Musk, the Foundation, the project or any other party in the cited sources, and this draft makes no such finding.[^1][^2][^3][^5]
