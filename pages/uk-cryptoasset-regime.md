# UK cryptoasset regime

The **UK cryptoasset regime** is the framework that brings cryptoasset
activities and [stablecoin](stablecoin.md) issuance in the United Kingdom
inside the authorisation system of the Financial Services and Markets Act
2000. It rests on the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000
(Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/102), made on 4 February 2026,
which specify new regulated activities including issuing a qualifying
stablecoin, safeguarding cryptoassets, operating a trading platform,
dealing, arranging deals and arranging staking. The Regulations come into
force in full on 25 October 2027.[^si] On 30 June 2026 the Financial
Conduct Authority (FCA) published its final rules in a package of policy
statements, and on 22 June 2026 the Bank of England published its policy
for systemic sterling stablecoins, which replaced proposed per-coin holding
limits with a temporary issuance limit of £40 billion per
coin.[^fca-overview][^boe-ps] Until the regime starts, FCA oversight of
crypto is limited to financial promotions and anti-money laundering
controls.[^fca-pr]

## Background

### Money laundering registration

Before the new regime, the main requirement on UK cryptoasset businesses
came from anti-money laundering law. The FCA became the supervisor of UK
cryptoasset businesses under the Money Laundering Regulations on 10 January
2020, and businesses providing in-scope services must register with it
before starting them.[^fca-who] Firms that were already trading had to
register by 10 January 2021. The FCA set up a temporary registration regime
under which firms that applied before 16 December 2020 and were still
awaiting a decision could keep trading until 9 July 2021.[^fca-trr] The FCA
states that its responsibility under this regime is limited to anti-money
laundering registration, supervision and enforcement, and that registration
does not give customers access to the Financial Ombudsman Service or the
Financial Services Compensation Scheme.[^fca-aml]

FCA figures as at 1 October 2026 record 427 applications received since
January 2020 and 393 determined, of which 68 (17%) were registered, 265
(67%) withdrawn, 46 (12%) rejected and 14 (4%) refused.[^fca-who]

### Financial promotions

HM Treasury brought promotions of qualifying cryptoassets into the existing
financial promotions regime through the Financial Services and Markets Act
2000 (Financial Promotion) (Amendment) Order 2023.[^hmt23] The FCA
published its rules in PS23/6 on cryptoasset financial promotions,
categorising cryptoassets as Restricted Mass Market Investments, and the
legislation entered into force on 8 October 2023.[^ps23-6] A promotion must
be made or approved by an authorised person, or fall within an exemption or
the route open to FCA-registered cryptoasset businesses. A promotion
outside those routes breaches section 21 of the Act, which is a criminal
offence punishable by up to two years' imprisonment, a fine, or
both.[^ps23-6]

## Legislative history

### Financial Services and Markets Act 2023

The Financial Services and Markets Act 2023 received Royal Assent on 29
June 2023. HM Treasury described it as containing powers confirming the
government's ability to specify cryptoasset activities in the Regulated
Activities Order and to designate activities under the designated
activities regime, and as providing the powers needed for a regime for
digital settlement assets used in payments.[^hmt23] Section 69 of the Act
amended the 2000 Act to insert a definition of "cryptoasset" in section 417
and extended the financial promotion and regulated activity provisions to
assets that are or represent cryptoassets.[^fsma23] The 2023 Act also
expanded the Bank of England's remit under the Banking Act 2009 to cover
digital settlement assets, including systemic stablecoins.[^boe-ps]

### HM Treasury consultations

HM Treasury published a consultation on the future regulatory framework for
cryptoassets in February 2023 and a response in October 2023. It received
131 responses, and about 80% were broadly supportive of the government's
approach. Of the respondents who answered the question on legislative
approach, 94% agreed or were generally in favour of bringing cryptoassets
within the framework of the 2000 Act rather than creating a separate
regime.[^hmt23]

In April 2025 the Treasury published a draft order, the Financial Services
and Markets Act 2000 (Regulated Activities and Miscellaneous Provisions)
(Cryptoassets) Order 2025, with a policy note.[^hmt25] The final instrument
took the form of regulations rather than an order.

### The Cryptoassets Regulations 2026

The Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) Regulations 2026
are SI 2026/102, made on 4 February 2026 and extending to England and
Wales, Scotland and Northern Ireland. Regulation 1 sets 25 October 2027 as
the "full commencement day". About three weeks after they were made, the
Regulations came into force for limited purposes: letting the FCA make
rules, give guidance and take preparatory steps, and letting applications
for permission be made and determined.[^si]

## Provisions

### Regulated activities

The Regulations amend the Regulated Activities Order to add a new Chapter
2B on cryptoassets. According to the explanatory note, the new regulated
activities are issuing qualifying stablecoin, safeguarding certain
cryptoassets, operating cryptoasset trading platforms, dealing in
cryptoassets as principal or as agent, arranging deals in cryptoassets, and
cryptoasset staking, and carrying them on requires authorisation under Part
4A of the 2000 Act.[^si] The Regulations also create designated activity
regimes for public offers and admissions to trading, and for market abuse,
and extend the financial promotion regime to the new activities.[^si]

| Activity | Article of the Regulated Activities Order |
|---|---|
| Issuing qualifying stablecoin | 9M |
| Safeguarding qualifying cryptoassets | 9N |
| Operating a qualifying cryptoasset trading platform | 9S |
| Dealing in qualifying cryptoassets as principal | 9T |
| Dealing in qualifying cryptoassets as agent | 9W |
| Arranging deals in qualifying cryptoassets | 9Y |
| Qualifying cryptoasset staking | 9Z6 |

Article numbers are those in the text of the Regulations as made.[^si]

### Stablecoin issuance

A person issues a qualifying stablecoin under article 9M if it offers the
coin for sale or subscription from an establishment in the United Kingdom,
the coin was created by it or a member of its group, and it carries on from
a UK establishment both the undertaking to redeem the coin and the holding
of assets to maintain its stable value. The article excludes minting itself
from the offering limb.[^si]

The FCA's final rules for non-systemic issuers are in PS26/10, published in
June 2026. They cover issuance, backing assets, redemption, safeguarding
and disclosures, and the FCA states they are intended to set a baseline for
stablecoin issuance.[^ps26-10] Main features are as follows.

- Backing assets. Issuers may hold core backing assets, consisting of
  short-term deposits and short-term government debt, and may use an
  expanded range of assets if they notify the FCA and meet further
  conditions. Issuers must hold at least 5% of the backing pool in
  on-demand deposits, and issuers using expanded assets must meet a backing
  asset composition requirement, calculated on every redemption day. After
  consultation the FCA removed the forward-looking estimate of daily
  redemptions from that calculation.[^ps26-10]
- Statutory trust. The backing asset pool must be held on statutory trust
  for the benefit of holders.[^ps26-10]
- Redemption. Holders may redeem at par. The final rules require a payment
  order within one business day (T+1), with the clock starting when the
  issuer receives the coin in its wallet, so that anti-money laundering
  checks happen before it starts.[^ps26-10]
- Interest. Issuers may not pass interest or income from the backing pool
  to holders. The FCA's rules do not prohibit third parties from paying
  rewards to their own customers from their own account, provided no
  interest or income from the backing pool is passed on.[^ps26-10]

The FCA's prudential rules for stablecoin issuers are in PS26/12, where the
operational risk K-factor requirement for stablecoin issuance was reduced
from 2% to 1% after consultation.[^ps26-12]

### Custody, trading platforms, intermediaries, lending and staking

PS26/11 sets the FCA's final rules for the other regulated activities:
operating a qualifying cryptoasset trading platform, dealing, arranging,
lending and borrowing, staking, safeguarding and an approach to
decentralised finance.[^ps26-11] The main points are these.

- Trading platforms and intermediaries. Principal dealers were removed from
  the pre-trade transparency requirements. On best execution, firms should
  check prices from at least three reliable UK authorised execution venues
  where possible, but need not execute on them.[^ps26-11] A firm operating
  a platform in the United Kingdom, or from overseas for UK consumers,
  needs FCA authorisation as a platform operator.[^ps26-11]
- Custody. Safeguarding is subject to the FCA's client assets rules in CASS
  17, adjusted for cryptoasset custody.[^ps26-11][^fca-overview]
- Lending and borrowing. These are not separate regulated activities. The
  FCA states that firms offering them may be carrying on dealing or
  arranging activities, and the rules apply when retail clients are
  involved. They include information requirements, express consent,
  appropriateness testing, extra record-keeping, and a ban on proprietary
  tokens in services to retail clients. The FCA notes that in cryptoasset
  lending the assets become the property of the recipient.[^ps26-11]
- Staking. The regulated activity is making arrangements on behalf of
  another person for qualifying cryptoasset staking, defined as the use of
  a qualifying cryptoasset in blockchain validation, including
  proof-of-stake consensus.[^si] The FCA's rules concentrate on retail
  understanding, with information and express consent before
  staking.[^ps26-11]
- Decentralised finance. The FCA states that it intends to apply its rules
  where there is an identifiable controlling entity, with separate guidance
  to follow on assessing decentralisation.[^ps26-11]

Further statements in the package are PS26/9 on admissions, disclosures and
market abuse, and PS26/13 on how the wider FCA Handbook, including the
Consumer Duty, applies to cryptoasset firms. A cost benefit analysis was
published with them.[^fca-overview] On 16 September 2026 the FCA published
PS26/18 on perimeter guidance, which states that existing registrations and
permissions will not convert automatically.[^ps26-18]

## Bank of England and systemic stablecoins

### Division of responsibility

The Bank of England states that within the United Kingdom regime the FCA
regulates the issuance, custody and admission to trading of UK-issued
qualifying stablecoins, and will in future regulate their use in payments.
Systemic stablecoins, meaning those widely used in payments that may pose
risks to financial stability, are regulated jointly by the Bank and the FCA
once recognised by HM Treasury.[^boe-ps] The Treasury recognises systemic
issuers after consulting the FCA, the Bank, the Payment Systems Regulator
and, where relevant, the Prudential Regulation Authority.[^ps26-10] A firm
may be systemic at launch or start under FCA-only regulation and move later
to joint regulation.[^ps26-10]

The Bank and the FCA published a joint approach document on 30 June 2026.
According to the Bank's page for the document, the Bank leads on areas
including backing assets, capital and reserves, safeguarding, failure
arrangements and the temporary issuance guardrail, while the FCA leads on
areas such as the Consumer Duty, conduct of business, financial crime and
the Senior Managers and Certification Regime, and the two coordinate on
operational resilience, governance and outsourcing.[^boe-joint] The FCA
described the document as explaining how responsibilities would be split
and how issuers may move from FCA supervision to joint
regulation.[^fca-joint]

### November 2025 consultation

The Bank published its consultation paper on 10 November 2025. It proposed
per-coin holding limits of £20,000 for individuals and £10 million for
businesses, with possible exemptions for businesses.[^boe-cp] The June 2026
policy statement records that the paper also proposed backing assets of 60%
short-term UK government debt and 40% unremunerated deposits at the
Bank.[^boe-ps]

### Policy statement of 22 June 2026

The policy statement, titled *Sterling-denominated systemic stablecoins*,
was published on 22 June 2026 together with a consultation on a draft Code
of Practice.[^boe-ps] Its main decisions were these.

- Holding limits dropped. The Bank concluded that holding limits were not
  justified as a proportionate or effective way to manage risks to credit
  provision, citing the operational challenges and costs respondents
  described. It replaced them with a temporary guardrail on issuance per
  systemic stablecoin, set initially at £40 billion.[^boe-ps]
- Calibration. The Bank states it used the same analytical approach as for
  the holding limits, so the guardrail should give the same protection
  against risks to credit provision. It compares the figure with daily
  Faster Payments and card scheme volumes of about £1.4 to £2.2 billion and
  says it is around 10% of average daily values processed by CHAPS. It says
  the £40 billion figure should not be read as the point at which the
  Treasury would recognise an issuer as systemic.[^boe-ps]
- Review. The Bank expects to loosen and ultimately remove the guardrail
  once it is satisfied the risk to credit provision has been
  mitigated.[^boe-ps]
- Backing assets. The mix was revised to 70% short-term UK government debt
  and 30% unremunerated Bank deposits in steady state. Issuers recognised
  as systemic at launch may hold up to 95% in government debt as they
  scale. Commercial bank deposits are not permitted as backing
  assets.[^boe-ps]
- Other terms. Issuers may not pay interest to coin holders. Capital is the
  higher of six months of operating expenses or the cost of the recovery
  and orderly wind-down plan. Redemption is to be in real time or by end of
  day, defined as 24 hours from receipt of a full request.[^boe-ps]
- Central bank liquidity facility. The Bank intends to publish design
  details of a liquidity backstop for systemic issuers in 2027.[^boe-ps]

The consultation on the draft Code of Practice closed on 22 September 2026.
The Bank intends to finalise the Code by the end of 2026, and to consult on
further supporting materials in 2027.[^boe-ps] This article found no final
Code of Practice as of October 2026.

## Implementation

The FCA states that its authorisation gateway opened on 30 September 2026
and closes on 28 February 2027, and that the regime starts on 25 October
2027.[^fca-pr] The FCA's overview page says firms that apply within the
window may be able to continue certain activities while the application is
assessed.[^fca-overview] The Regulations include a transitional pathway for
existing businesses.[^si] The FCA also stated that a consultation on
resolution for cryptoasset firms and on financial crime guidance was
planned for later in 2026.[^fca-overview]

The Bank's Code of Practice applies only to issuers that HM Treasury has
recognised as systemic.[^boe-ps] This article found no report of such a
recognition as of October 2026.

## Criticism

### Holding limits and pace

When the Bank proposed holding limits in November 2025, crypto firms and
trade groups argued they would be hard to police and could hold back
innovation, noting that stablecoins move freely on secondary markets. They
also objected to the 40% unremunerated deposit requirement. The Block
reported that Stand With Crypto UK's director said almost 85,000 people had
signed a petition.[^block-breeden] The Bank's own policy statement records
the main objections: significant operational concerns, a risk of pushing
issuance to non-sterling stablecoins, investment not being justified for a
temporary tool, and a business limit seen as too restrictive.[^boe-ps]

The House of Lords Financial Services Regulation Committee reported on 3
June 2026 in *Stablecoins: waiting for regulation*. It recommended that the
Bank not introduce holding limits in advance, and said the caps would be
commercially damaging and hard to enforce. It also criticised the 40%
unremunerated deposit proposal and asked the FCA to reconsider its K-factor
requirement for stablecoins. Its chair, Baroness Noakes, was reported as
saying the United Kingdom was lagging the United States and the European
Union but was "now moving in the right direction".[^block-lords] The Bank's
policy statement says its approach responds to the committee's
recommendations on pre-committed timelines.[^boe-ps]

After the policy statement, Katie Harries of Coinbase said the rules
"deliver among the strongest stablecoin regimes in the world", but
questioned how long the "temporary" issuance cap would last and said the
United Kingdom was the only country capping issuance of stablecoins in its
own currency. She also asked whether stablecoins could be used for
settlement in core wholesale markets.[^block-boe]

In consultation on the FCA rules, a large share of respondents disagreed
with parts of the proposals. For backing asset composition, 43% of
respondents to the question did not support the proposal, and for the T+1
redemption requirement 48% did not. The FCA responded by simplifying the
first and changing the start of the redemption clock, as described
above.[^ps26-10]

### The Bank's stated concerns

The Bank states that it continues to believe there are risks to credit
provision from large, rapid outflows of bank deposits into stablecoins, and
that the guardrail is a transitional measure. It acknowledges that the
guardrail brings a new risk of a coin trading above par if demand exceeds
supply, which it considers manageable. It also notes that holding
government debt as backing creates an interdependency with the gilt market,
because an issuer facing rapid redemptions might have to sell assets at a
discount.[^boe-ps] Sarah Breeden, a Deputy Governor, told the Lords
committee in March 2026 that the Bank was "genuinely open to other ways of
achieving the objective", and that enforcing temporary caps required
systems whose cost had to be justified.[^block-breeden]

The Bank also rejected industry calls to broaden eligible backing assets to
commercial bank deposits and money market funds, citing financial and
operational risks, and rejected remuneration of Bank deposits, on the
ground that stablecoins used as payment instruments are not involved in
monetary policy transmission.[^boe-ps] The FCA likewise declined to add low
volatility money market funds to its backing assets, saying they could
increase the volatility of the backing pool.[^ps26-10]

### Comparison with other jurisdictions

The FCA says its ban on passing backing asset income to holders is in line
with the approach in the European Union and the United States.[^ps26-10]
The Lords committee, as reported, noted that no other jurisdiction imposed
holding limits of the kind first proposed, and Coinbase made the comparison
about issuance caps noted above.[^block-lords][^block-boe] For the United
States, see the [GENIUS Act](genius-act.md).
