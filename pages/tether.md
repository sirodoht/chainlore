# Tether

**Tether** (USDT) is a stablecoin issued by Tether Limited and related
companies, designed to hold a value of one US dollar. It began in 2014 as
Realcoin, a token on the Omni Layer of [Bitcoin](bitcoin.md), and now
runs on many blockchains, including [Ethereum](ethereum.md) and
Tron.[^rebrand][^protocols] Tether shares owners and executives with the
exchange Bitfinex.[^cftc-order] Its claim that each token was backed by a
dollar in reserve led to settlements with the New York Attorney General
in 2021 and the US Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) in the same
year.[^nyag-2021][^cftc] Its reserves are now held mostly in US Treasury
bills, with smaller amounts in gold, bitcoin and secured
loans.[^fy-2025]

## History

### Realcoin

On 9 July 2014 Brock Pierce announced Realcoin, a token to be backed one
for one by US dollars held in reserve. It was built on Mastercoin, a
protocol that adds a layer on top of the Bitcoin blockchain. The
announcement also involved Reeve Collins, an advertising entrepreneur,
and Craig Sellars of Mastercoin.[^realcoin] On 20 November 2014 the
project was renamed Tether as it entered private beta. Collins was its
chief executive and Sellars, a co-founder, was also chief technology
officer of Mastercoin. Bitfinex, then based in Hong Kong, was among the
first exchanges to sign on.[^rebrand] Pierce's founding team, which also
included William Quigley, stepped away from the project early on.[^ardoino-profile]

### Bitfinex and shared management

Tether and Bitfinex have overlapping owners and managers. The CFTC found
that they "shared common ownership, leadership, management, employees and
operational resources", with the same individuals serving as chief
executive, chief financial officer, chief technology officer and general
counsel of both.[^cftc-order] Jean-Louis van der Velde was chief
executive of Tether until October 2023 and remained chief executive of
Bitfinex.[^ardoino-ceo] Giancarlo Devasini, who was running Bitfinex in
2014, was by 2023 Tether's chief financial officer.[^ardoino-profile] Paolo
Ardoino, whom Devasini hired after they met in 2014, became chief
technology officer of both companies, and was promoted to chief executive
of Tether on 13 October 2023 while remaining Bitfinex's chief technology
officer.[^ardoino-ceo][^ardoino-profile]
Stuart Hoegner was general counsel to both.[^hoegner]

In April 2017 Bitfinex's parent iFinex and Tether sued Wells Fargo, which
had stopped processing outgoing wire transfers from four Taiwanese banks
used by Bitfinex. The suit was withdrawn on 11 April, about a week after
it was filed.[^wells-fargo][^wells-fargo-drop]
The CFTC later found that from May 2017 Tether held some reserve money in
an account in the name of its general counsel, and from June 2017 in
Bitfinex's own accounts.[^cftc-order] In November 2017 the Paradise
Papers leak showed that Devasini and Philip Potter, both Bitfinex
executives, were also beneficial owners of Tether. The companies said
they had already disclosed this in an April 2017 court
filing.[^icij]

### Auditor and banking problems

Tether hired the accounting firm Friedman LLP in May 2017.[^friedman-sec]
In September 2017 a preliminary report by Friedman stated that Tether held
$442.9 million in cash, matching the tokens then in circulation. It was
not an audit.[^friedman]
The CFTC later found that the review covered a single date chosen in
advance by Tether, and that Bitfinex moved $382 million into Tether's
newly opened bank account that day.[^cftc-order] On 27 January 2018
Tether said its relationship with Friedman had "dissolved" and that an
audit was "unattainable in a reasonable time frame".[^friedman][^friedman-sec]

Bitfinex from about 2014, and Tether from October 2017, relied on a
payment processor registered in Panama, which the New York Attorney
General identified as Crypto Capital, to hold customer and reserve money.[^nyag-2021][^cftc-order] In
April 2018 media reported that authorities had seized funds held by the
processor, and Bitfinex had growing difficulty withdrawing money it had
placed there.[^cftc-order] In November 2018 Tether moved $625 million of reserve funds to Bitfinex, later formalised
as a credit facility.[^cftc-order]

### New York and CFTC cases

On 25 April 2019 the New York Attorney General announced a court order
against iFinex, alleging that Bitfinex had lost about $850 million held
with Crypto Capital and had used Tether's reserves to cover the
shortfall without telling investors. Bitfinex said the filings were
written in bad faith and contained false assertions.[^nyag-2019] In an affidavit days later, Hoegner
said that cash and short-term securities backed about 74% of tethers in
circulation.[^hoegner]

On 23 February 2021 the companies settled. They agreed to pay $18.5
million, stop trading with New Yorkers, and for two years publish
quarterly reports on the categories of assets backing USDT. They neither
admitted nor denied the Attorney General's findings, which said Tether's
claim of full dollar backing had been false and that for periods it held
no reserves.[^nyag-2021][^nyag-settlement]

On 15 October 2021 the CFTC fined Tether $41 million and Bitfinex $1.5
million. It found that between 2 September 2016 and 1 November 2018
Tether held enough fiat in its own bank accounts to back the tokens in
circulation on only 27.6% of days. It also found that the reserves had
never been audited. Tether settled without admitting or denying the
findings.[^cftc][^cftc-order] Tether told the CFTC that it had
never failed to honour a redemption request.[^cftc-order]

### Chains

Tether first issued tokens on Omni. On 11 September 2017 it launched
ERC-20 tokens on Ethereum, with contracts built by
Ethfinex.[^erc20] On 4 March 2019 it announced a TRC-20 version on Tron;
at that time almost all USDT was still on Omni.[^tron] Tether later
issued USDT on more than a dozen chains, including Solana, Avalanche,
TON, Aptos and NEAR.[^protocols]

On 17 August 2023 Tether stopped issuing USDT on Omni, Kusama and the
Bitcoin Cash SLP protocol, citing low use.[^omni-end] In July 2025 it
said it would end redemptions on these chains and on EOS and Algorand
from 1 September 2025, and freeze the remaining tokens.[^legacy] In
September 2026 Ardoino said USDT was "coming home" to Bitcoin through
Utexo, a Tether-backed project using the RGB protocol.[^bitcoin-return] On
22 October 2026 Utexo opened early access to a bridged version of USDT on
a Bitcoin test network, with a public launch scheduled for 28
November 2026.[^utexo-mint]

### Relocation and the US market

On 13 January 2025 Tether said it would move its group headquarters to
El Salvador, after two Tether companies received licences from the
country's digital asset regulator. Most of its entities had reportedly
been incorporated in the British Virgin Islands.[^el-salvador]

The US GENIUS Act, signed on 18 July 2025, set federal rules for
stablecoins, requiring them to be backed by liquid assets such as
dollars and short-term Treasuries, with monthly reserve
disclosure.[^genius] On 12 September 2025 Tether announced USAT, a
separate dollar stablecoin for the US market, to be issued by the
federally chartered Anchorage Digital Bank, with reserves managed by
Cantor Fitzgerald. Bo Hines, formerly executive director of the White
House crypto council, was named chief executive of the US
business.[^usat-announce] USAT launched on 27 January
2026.[^usat-launch]

USDT itself is a foreign-issued stablecoin under the Act. As of July
2026, CoinDesk estimated from Tether's disclosures that up to a quarter of
its reserves were in assets,
such as gold, loans and bitcoin, that did not meet the Act's standards,
and lawyers disagreed on whether Tether must comply by January 2027 or
July 2028.[^genius-countdown]

## Reserves and attestations

Tether did not publish a breakdown of its reserves until May 2021,
under the New York settlement. As of 31 March 2021 about 76% of reserves
were in cash, cash equivalents and commercial paper, with commercial
paper making up 65% of that group. Secured loans were 12.55%.[^reserves-2021]
After the 2022 market stress Tether reduced its commercial paper, and on
13 October 2022 said it held none, having replaced it with US Treasury
bills.[^depeg-fortune][^cp-zero]

On 17 May 2023 Tether said it would put up to 15% of realised profits
into bitcoin. It also held gold in its reserves.[^btc-policy] By the end
of 2025 its attestation listed $122 billion in direct Treasury holdings,
$17.4 billion in gold and $8.4 billion in bitcoin, against $186.5
billion in liabilities.[^fy-2025] In the second quarter of 2026 the
value of its bitcoin and gold fell, and its excess reserves halved from
$8.2 billion to $4.1 billion even though it added 14 tonnes of
gold.[^q2-2026]

Reserve reports were prepared as attestations, which check balances at a
single point in time, not as audits. Two Cayman Islands firms, Moore
Cayman and MHA Cayman, produced them until 2022, when Tether moved to BDO
Italia.[^bdo] In November 2025 S&P Global Ratings cut its assessment of
USDT's ability to hold its peg from "constrained" to "weak", its lowest
score, citing bitcoin exposure and limited disclosure. Tether rejected
the assessment.[^sp-2025]

On 24 March 2026 Tether said it had engaged a Big Four firm for its first
full audit; the *Financial Times* reported that it was
KPMG.[^audit-hire][^kpmg] On 13 August 2026 Tether said KPMG had issued
an unqualified opinion on Tether International's financial statements
for the year ended 31 December 2025, which a KPMG spokesperson
confirmed. Tether had not said whether it would publish KPMG's
findings.[^audit-done]

## Profits and investments

Tether reported group net profit of $13 billion for 2024, about $7
billion of it from its Treasury and repo holdings, and more than $10
billion for 2025.[^profit-2024][^fy-2025] It invests profits and excess
reserves through Tether Capital, which describes stakes in artificial
intelligence, energy, commodities, digital media and sport.[^tether-capital]

In September 2025 Bloomberg reported that Tether sought to raise up to
$20 billion at a valuation of about $500 billion.[^fundraise] In
February 2026 the *Financial Times* reported that advisers had cut the
figure to about $5 billion after investor resistance.[^fundraise-cut]
Tether, which owned more than 10% of Juventus, made an all-cash bid for
the club's controlling stake in December 2025. Exor, the majority
owner, rejected it.[^juventus][^juventus-reject]

## Incidents and controversies

- **Market manipulation research.** In a paper published in the
  *Journal of Finance* in 2020, John Griffin and Amin Shams reported that
  Tether purchases during the 2017 boom tended to follow
  market falls, came from a single entity, and preceded large bitcoin
  price rises, which they found consistent with unbacked money
  inflating prices.[^griffin] Tether called the 2019 version of the
  paper "flawed" and said tokens were issued on demand, not to move
  prices.[^griffin-response] A 2018 study by Wang Chun Wei found that
  Tether issuance did not predict later bitcoin returns.[^wei]
- **2022 run.** After the collapse of [Terra](terra.md)'s UST, USDT
  fell to about $0.95 on 12 May 2022 before recovering. Tether said it
  processed about $10 billion of redemptions from 11 to 23 May.[^depeg-fortune][^depeg-decrypt]
- **Celsius lawsuit.** Celsius sued Tether over the liquidation of about
  40,000 bitcoin it had pledged as loan collateral in June
  2022.[^celsius] Tether settled for $299.5 million in October
  2025.[^celsius-settle]
- **Illicit use.** In January 2024 the UN Office on Drugs and Crime
  said USDT on Tron was popular with money launderers in East and Southeast Asia.
  Tether said the report ignored its law enforcement
  work.[^unodc] On 28 September 2026 Democrats on a Senate subcommittee,
  led by its top Democrat, Senator Richard Blumenthal, said USDT had become "a significant
  financial lifeline" within Iran's shadow banking network. Ardoino
  replied that Tether's actions had cut Iran-linked stablecoin volumes
  by 94%.[^iran-report][^iran-response]
- **Political ties.** In October 2026 Blumenthal wrote to Cantor
  Fitzgerald, which manages Tether's US reserves, asking about its
  relationship with Tether. Cantor was formerly led by Commerce Secretary
  Howard Lutnick and is now run by his son Brandon.[^blumenthal-cantor][^audit-hire]

## Freezing and law enforcement

USDT contracts let Tether freeze addresses, blocking transfers from
them.[^freeze-israel] In December 2023 it began freezing addresses on the
US Treasury sanctions list voluntarily.[^freeze-2023] With Tron and TRM
Labs it formed the T3 Financial Crime Unit, which said it had frozen
$300 million in its first year. Ardoino said Tether worked with more
than 280 law enforcement agencies.[^t3] By July 2026 Tether had frozen
about $475 million in USDT linked to Iran's central bank.[^iran-freeze]
