# Payment Services Act (Japan)

The **Payment Services Act** (Act No. 59 of 2009) is the Japanese statute
that regulates payment businesses including non-bank funds transfers. Its
crypto-asset exchange registration regime took effect in 2017; a separate
framework for currency-linked [stablecoins](stablecoin.md) took effect in
2023. Amendments in 2016, 2019, 2022 and 2025 added a registration regime
for crypto exchanges, tightened custody rules after a major exchange hack,
created a category of "electronic payment instruments" for yen- and
foreign-currency-linked stablecoins, and added a lighter registration
category for intermediaries. The Financial Services Agency (FSA) supervises
the regime.[^act][^fsa-slides][^chambers] A further amendment passed by the
Diet in July 2026 will move the regulation of crypto-assets out of the Act
and into the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act.[^amt-2026][^adnkronos]

## Background

The Act was enacted on 17 June 2009 and promulgated on 24 June
2009.[^fsa-2009] It regulates prepaid payment instruments, funds transfer
services by persons other than deposit-taking institutions, and the
clearing of exchange transactions between such institutions. Its stated aim
is to ensure that payment services are appropriately provided and users are
protected.[^act]

Crypto-assets entered the Act after the collapse of the Mt. Gox exchange.
The FSA names the Mt. Gox incident (about US$480 million) as the trigger
for the first legal reform of 2016, alongside the G7 Elmau Summit leaders'
declaration, which called for appropriate regulation, and the Financial
Action Task Force guidance of June 2015, which recommended registration or
licensing of virtual currency exchanges.[^fsa-slides]

## Legislative history

### 2016 amendment: virtual currency exchanges

The 2016 amendment of the Payment Services Act and of the Act on Prevention
of Transfer of Criminal Proceeds was enforced in April 2017. It introduced
a registration system for virtual currency exchange service providers,
identity verification on account opening, and user-protection requirements
covering minimum capital, provision of information to customers,
segregation of assets and system security.[^fsa-slides] From 1 April 2017
the registration regime operated through the competent Local Finance
Bureau. The FSA reported that as of the end of September 2017, 11
applicants were registered and 19 were under examination.[^fsa-2017]

### 2019 amendment: crypto-assets and custody

In January 2018 a large exchange was hacked and a significant amount of
customers' virtual currency was lost, according to the law firm K&L Gates.
On-site FSA inspections that followed found that many exchanges' internal
management and operating systems were insufficient.[^kl-gates] The FSA
identifies the incident as the Coincheck incident (about US$530
million).[^fsa-slides] An FSA study group met 11 times between April and
December 2018 and reported in December 2018.[^fsa-slides][^kl-gates]

The Diet passed the resulting bill, amending the Payment Services Act and
the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act, on 31 May 2019, and it was
promulgated on 7 June 2019.[^kl-gates] The FSA gives May 2020 as the date
it was enforced.[^fsa-slides] Its main changes were:[^kl-gates]

- The term "virtual currency" in the Act was renamed "crypto-assets" (*angō
  shisan*), to follow more widely used international terminology.[^kl-gates]
- Holding crypto-assets on behalf of clients was added to the regulated
  crypto-asset exchange business, so custodians had to register.[^kl-gates]
- Crypto-asset margin trading and other derivatives were brought under the
  derivatives rules of the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act.[^kl-gates]
- Rights recorded or transferred on a distributed ledger, such as many
  token offerings, were brought into the securities regime as "electronic
  recording transfer rights".[^kl-gates]
- Market manipulation and other unfair trading practices involving
  crypto-assets were prohibited. K&L Gates notes that the amendment did not
  include an insider-trading prohibition, because FSA officials said it was
  difficult to identify the issuer of a crypto-asset or the information
  material to its price.[^kl-gates]

The FSA describes the requirements for registered exchanges after this
reform as including keeping at least 95 per cent of users' crypto-assets
offline, managing users' money in a trust, segregating assets, and handling
only crypto-assets reviewed by a self-regulatory organisation and notified
to the FSA.[^fsa-slides] The English text of the Act defines crypto-assets
as property value that can be used with unspecified persons to pay for
goods or services and can be bought and sold with unspecified persons, or
that can be exchanged for such property, and that can be transferred by
electronic data processing. The definition excludes yen, foreign currencies
and currency-denominated assets.[^act]

### 2022 amendment: stablecoins

The stablecoin amendment was promulgated on 10 June 2022 as the Act
Partially Amending the Payment Services Act, and its Cabinet Orders and
Cabinet Office Orders were finalised on 26 May 2023 and enforced on 1 June
2023.[^fsa-wr540] The FSA presents it as the third legal reform of the
regime, prompted by the wider use of stablecoins abroad and by the
Financial Stability Board's high-level recommendations. It also amended the
Banking Act and the Trust Business Act.[^fsa-slides]

Clifford Chance, writing in June 2022, tied the framework to the
international discussion that followed the announcement of the Diem (Libra)
project, and noted that the collapse of TerraUSD in May 2022 had sharpened
regulators' calls for a dedicated framework.[^cc-2022]

### 2025 amendment: intermediaries

The Act Partially Amending the Payment Services Act was enacted in June
2025 as Act No. 66 of 2025.[^fsa-wr642][^amt-2026] It extended payment
regulation to businesses providing cross-border payment collection
services.[^fsa-wr642] Chambers reports that a bill submitted to the Diet in
March 2025 proposed a registration category for businesses that only
intermediate trades in crypto-assets or electronic payment instruments, on
the ground that full registration as an exchange service provider was
considered excessively stringent for them.[^chambers] The FSA promulgated
Cabinet Order and Cabinet Office Orders implementing the amendment on 22
May 2026. They covered provisions on electronic payment instruments and
crypto-assets, funds transfer services, and banks and insurers and their
subsidiaries.[^fsa-wr688] The FSA confirmed that the amended Act and these
implementing provisions took effect on 1 June 2026.[^fsa-2026-orders]

### 2026 amendment: moving crypto-assets to the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act

The Cabinet approved the Bill for Partially Amending the Financial
Instruments and Exchange Act and the Payment Services Act on 10 April 2026
and submitted it to the 221st session of the Diet. Anderson Mori &
Tomotsune describes four pillars, the first being a review of crypto-asset
regulation. The bill builds a disclosure regime, business regulation and
unfair-trading rules for crypto-assets in the Financial Instruments and
Exchange Act, deletes the provisions on crypto-asset exchange service
providers from the Payment Services Act, and deletes the crypto-asset
intermediary provisions introduced by the 2025 amendment. Regulation of
crypto-asset transactions would then be centralised under the Financial
Instruments and Exchange Act, with many details left to subordinate
legislation.[^amt-2026]

The House of Councillors approved the bill at a plenary meeting on 15 July
2026; the FSA's legislative record confirms enactment on that
date.[^fsa-221][^adnkronos][^coindesk-jul] The Adnkronos report states that
the new law raises the maximum penalties for unregistered crypto trading
businesses to 10 years in prison and a fine of 10 million yen, up from
three years and 3 million yen, and that the law adds rules against insider
trading in crypto-assets.[^adnkronos] CoinDesk reported that the new rules
were expected to take effect in 2027, and that lawmakers also approved a
framework for a flat 20 per cent tax rate on crypto income, not expected to
apply before 2028.[^coindesk-jul] This article could not confirm an exact
commencement date as of October 2026. The Anderson Mori summary describes
deletions limited to crypto-asset provisions.[^amt-2026]

## Provisions

### Role of the Financial Services Agency

The Act delegates the Prime Minister's authority under it to the
Commissioner of the Financial Services Agency, who may delegate part of it
to the heads of Local Finance Bureaus.[^act] Registration of a crypto-asset
exchange service provider is made with the Prime Minister, and no one may
provide the service without it.[^act] The FSA also publishes the
implementing orders and the guidelines, and its officials have presented
the regime to international bodies as covering financial stability, user
protection and anti-money-laundering as three priorities.[^fsa-slides]

### Crypto-assets other than stablecoins

Until the 2026 law takes effect, crypto-asset exchange service providers
must register under the Act. The English text defines the regulated service
as purchase, sale or exchange of crypto-assets, intermediary, brokerage or
agency services for those acts, management of users' money in connection
with them, and management of crypto-assets on behalf of another
person.[^act] The FSA's summary of the user-protection regime is given
under the 2019 amendment above.[^fsa-slides]

### Electronic payment instruments

The 2022 amendment treats currency-linked stablecoins as "electronic
payment instruments". Clifford Chance quotes the definition as
currency-denominated assets that can be used for remittance and settlement
to unspecified persons and transferred using an electronic information
processing organisation. The amendment does not require a distributed
ledger.[^cc-2022] Chambers reports that stablecoins redeemable for fiat
currency are regulated as electronic payment instruments, while stablecoins
not backed by fiat currency, such as DAI, continue to be treated as
crypto-assets.[^chambers]

### Who may issue

Issuance in Japan is limited to licensed banks, funds transfer service
providers and trust companies.[^cc-2022][^fsa-slides] The FSA's December
2022 explanation described the redemption protection for each
route:[^fsa-slides]

- Banks may issue stablecoins as deposits, covered by deposit insurance in
  the same way as other deposits.[^fsa-slides]
- Funds transfer service providers may issue them as claims, and must
  secure the obligation through deposits with official depositaries, bank
  guarantees or entrusted safe assets such as bank deposits and government
  bonds.[^fsa-slides]
- Trust companies may issue them as trust beneficiary rights, and must hold
  all trust assets as bank deposits under the original framework.[^fsa-slides]

The 2025 amendment, effective from 1 June 2026, broadened permissible
backing for specified trust beneficiary rights to include certain
government bonds and term deposits that permit early cancellation, subject
to investment limits and principal-protection
requirements.[^fsa-2026-orders]

Chambers reports that stablecoins issued under the Type II funds transfer
service provider licence are subject to a cap of 1 million yen per issuance
or redemption transaction.[^chambers] SBI states that its trust-bank
stablecoin is not subject to the 1 million yen transaction and balance
limits that apply to funds transfer type stablecoins.[^block-jpysc]

### Intermediaries

Clifford Chance observed that before the amendment, issuance was already
restricted but the transfer and management of stablecoins were not
regulated. The amendment added a licence for intermediaries, bound by
anti-money-laundering codes of conduct.[^cc-2022] The FSA says
intermediaries must register as electronic payment instrument exchange
service providers. It describes requirements to contract with issuers on
sharing liability for losses and, subject to points it described in
December 2022 as still under consideration, not to deal in stablecoins
unless they have clear transfer rules, meet anti-money-laundering
requirements and offer protections in an issuer's failure.[^fsa-slides] The
FSA's 2023 notice of the final orders adds that these intermediaries become
"specified business operators" under the criminal proceeds law, and that a
travel rule applies to transfers of stablecoins and
crypto-assets.[^fsa-wr540]

### Algorithmic stablecoins

Clifford Chance reports that stablecoins that stabilise value through
algorithms fall outside the electronic payment instrument framework and
continue under the rules for crypto-assets and security tokens.[^cc-2022]
The FSA's December 2022 presentation said that self-described stablecoins
such as algorithmic ones, naming Terra, and non-redemption ones, naming
DAI, were categorised in the same regulatory category as Bitcoin in
Japan.[^fsa-slides] See [Terra](terra.md).

### Foreign stablecoins

Clifford Chance noted in 2022 that intermediaries were not expressly barred
from dealing in stablecoins issued overseas. It reported that an FSA
working group had suggested the overseas issuer should maintain assets in
Japan matching the stablecoins distributed there, which would make use of
foreign stablecoins difficult if adopted, and that other
investor-protection measures could substitute.[^cc-2022] Whether specific
foreign stablecoins would be allowed was left to subordinate legislation.
In May 2026 the FSA promulgated a Cabinet Office Order amendment that
widened the scope of foreign trust beneficial interests treated as
electronic payment instruments.[^fsa-wr688]

## Implementation

### JPYC

JPYC Inc. was reported to have registered as a funds transfer service
provider with the FSA in August 2025 and to have launched a yen stablecoin,
JPYC, on 27 October 2025.[^block-jpyc] The company says it holds 100 per
cent reserves in yen deposits and Japanese government bonds, and The Block
described it as the country's first legally recognised yen-backed
stablecoin.[^block-jpyc] In a 27 October 2026 interview with NADA NEWS,
chief executive Noritaka Okabe said circulation was well below internal
targets and pointed to the 1 million yen limit on each issuance or
redemption as a source of friction for business users.[^nada-jpyc]

### USDC and SBI VC Trade

On 4 March 2025 SBI VC Trade announced that it had completed registration
to handle [USDC](usdc.md), which the company described as the first in the
country, with a beta for selected users from 12 March.[^block-sbi-usdc] In
a release dated 25 March 2025, Circle described USDC as the first global
dollar stablecoin approved under Japan's framework, with full-scale service
on SBI VC Trade due to begin on 26 March and Binance Japan, bitbank and
bitFlyer planning to follow.[^circle-usdc]

### SBI's JPYSC

SBI Group and Startale launched JPYSC on 24 June 2026. SBI Shinsei Trust
Bank issues it and SBI VC Trade distributes it. SBI says it is the first
stablecoin of its kind classified as an electronic payment instrument under
the Act, and availability is limited to SBI VC Trade accounts until its
regulatory and tax treatment is clarified.[^block-jpysc]

### Megabank project

On 10 June 2026 MUFG, SMBC and Mizuho said in a joint statement that they
plan to issue a stablecoin under a trust arrangement, with the banks as
joint settlors and a trust bank as trustee, and aim for live commercial
transactions during fiscal year 2026, which ends in March 2027. The FSA
backed the project in November 2025 as a test of whether it can operate
lawfully under existing rules, within its FinTech proof-of-concept
hub.[^megabanks-june][^block-megabanks]

On 16 October 2026 the banks, Mitsubishi UFJ Trust and Banking Corporation
and TISI announced that they had conducted a proof of concept on 17–23 July
2026, issuing stablecoins as specified trust beneficiary rights under the
Act and testing cross-border payments between Mitsubishi Corporation
locations. Their announcement still described practical deployment as work
in progress, distinct from the completed trial.[^megabanks-oct]

## Criticism

JPYC's chief executive says the transaction limit on funds-transfer-type
stablecoins restricts business use.[^nada-jpyc] Anderson Mori & Tomotsune
notes that the 2026 bill will impose stricter requirements and new
compliance costs on crypto firms, and that many details remain to be set by
subordinate legislation.[^amt-2026]
