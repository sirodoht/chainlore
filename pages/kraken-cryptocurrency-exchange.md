Kraken is a cryptocurrency exchange that operates under the Kraken name through Payward companies. The SEC describes Payward Ventures, Inc. and Payward Trading, Ltd. as both commonly known as Kraken.[^imp-28-1] Kraken's November 2025 company announcement, issued by Payward, Inc. (dba Kraken), says the company was founded in 2011 and that clients trade more than 450 digital assets, along with U.S. futures, U.S.-listed stocks and ETFs, and fiat currencies.[^imp-28-2]

## History

Kraken states that it pioneered proof-of-reserves in 2014.[^4]

According to the SEC, Kraken offered staking to the general public from 2019 through a pooled staking-as-a-service program that advertised annual returns of as much as 21 percent.[^2]

In February 2023, the SEC announced a settlement with the two Kraken entities over the staking program. Without admitting or denying the SEC's allegations, they agreed to immediately cease offering or selling securities through crypto asset staking services or staking programs and to pay $30 million in disgorgement, prejudgment interest and civil penalties. They also consented to a final judgment, subject to court approval, that would permanently enjoin them from violating Section 5 of the Securities Act of 1933 and from offering or selling securities through staking services or programs.[^imp-28-1]

In November 2023, the SEC charged Payward Inc. and Payward Ventures Inc., together known as Kraken, with operating as an unregistered securities exchange, broker, dealer and clearing agency. The SEC alleged that Kraken commingled customer and company funds.[^imp-28-4]

Kraken announced in March 2025 that SEC staff had agreed in principle to dismiss the lawsuit with prejudice.[^imp-28-5]

In November 2025, Payward, Inc. (dba Kraken) announced that it had confidentially submitted a draft registration statement on Form S-1 with the SEC for a proposed initial public offering of its common stock. The announcement said the number of shares and the price range had not been determined.[^imp-28-2]

## Technology

### Proof of reserves

Kraken describes proof of reserves as a cryptographic process that lets clients verify, independently and privately, that their assets are included in a third-party accountancy firm's snapshot of the platform's liabilities.[^imp-28-3] Kraken's November 2025 company announcement says it was the first crypto platform to conduct proof of reserves.[^imp-28-2]

- Kraken describes proof-of-reserves as a third-party accountancy snapshot of client liabilities and on-chain holdings. It says individual balances are combined into a Merkle tree, that clients receive a personal Merkle proof to confirm inclusion, and that the accountancy firm confirms on-chain holdings exceed total client balances. The firm is not named in the post.[^4]
- Kraken's self-reported report, attested as of December 31, 2025, covers BTC, ETH, SOL, USDC, USDT, XRP and ADA and says client assets are backed 1:1 and beyond.[^4]
- Kraken says it will publish proof-of-reserves reports quarterly and expand asset coverage.[^4] A 2022 press release announced proof-of-reserves audits and named Armanino as the auditor.[^12]
- A weak source notes that a reserve check does not establish a company's complete financial position.[^16] The SEC's alleged commingling of customer and company funds is a separate matter from a reserve snapshot.[^1]
