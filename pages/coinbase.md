# Coinbase

Coinbase is a cryptocurrency business providing trading, custody, and
developer services. Founded by Brian Armstrong and Fred Ehrsam, it began
as a hosted bitcoin wallet in 2012 and listed on Nasdaq in 2021. Its
activities include the [Base](base.md) network and a commercial relationship
with Circle, the issuer of [USDC](usdc.md).[^yc][^listing][^about][^circle]

## History

Armstrong joined Y Combinator's summer 2012 programme with a project
initially called Bitbank. Y Combinator's retrospective describes Coinbase's
early product as a hosted bitcoin wallet intended to simplify access for
nontechnical users, and records Ehrsam joining as co-founder. On 14 April
2021, Armstrong announced Coinbase's Nasdaq listing.[^yc][^listing]

## Products and custody

Coinbase describes its business as serving consumers through financial
applications, institutions through Coinbase Prime, and developers through
the Coinbase Developer Platform. Its product catalogue includes spot and
derivatives trading, custody, staking, and wallet infrastructure. These
services should be distinguished from the operation of an underlying public
blockchain: holding an account with a company and holding a key-controlled
onchain account are different arrangements.[^about][^wallets]

Coinbase announced Base on 23 February 2023 as an Ethereum layer 2,
initially built using the OP Stack. The announcement described the network
as a way to support applications beyond the exchange itself. The separate
[Base article](base.md) covers its technical and governance history.[^base]

Coinbase closed its acquisition of the crypto derivatives exchange Deribit
on 14 August 2025. Its help centre records completion of the integration
of Deribit and Coinbase International Exchange on 1 October 2026, including
migration of International Exchange institutional accounts, positions, and
balances to the combined platform.[^deribit][^integration]

## USDC relationship

In August 2023 Circle and Coinbase announced a revised arrangement: Circle
would assume full responsibility for USDC issuance and governance, Coinbase
would take an equity stake in Circle, and the companies would continue
sharing interest income from reserves. This separates the role of issuer
from Coinbase's distribution and commercial interests.[^circle]

## Incidents and regulatory proceedings

On 27 February 2025 the SEC announced a joint stipulation to dismiss its
civil enforcement action against Coinbase. The commission expressly said
its decision reflected a change in regulatory approach, rather than an
assessment of the merits of the allegations.[^sec]

On 15 May 2025 Coinbase disclosed that attackers had bribed overseas
support personnel to obtain customer information for impersonation scams.
The company said the stolen information did not include passwords or
private keys, refused a US$20 million ransom demand, and committed to
reimbursing customers tricked into sending funds to the attackers. Those
statements describe Coinbase's account and commitments at disclosure.[^incident]

In October 2026, Coinbase said an incident at analytics provider Amplitude
had exposed contact information for a small subset of its customers.
CoinDesk reported the company's statement on 28 October UTC: Coinbase said
its own systems had not been accessed without authorisation, customer funds
were safe, and it was notifying affected customers. As of 31 October,
the cited reports did not establish the number of Coinbase users
affected.[^amplitude][^amplitude-decrypt]
