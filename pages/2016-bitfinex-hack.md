The 2016 Bitfinex hack was the theft of approximately 120,000 bitcoin from the virtual currency exchange Bitfinex in August 2016.[^1] Bitfinex spread the loss across customer accounts and compensated customers with BFX tokens.[^2][^3] In February 2022, US authorities arrested two people accused of laundering the stolen bitcoin and seized more than 94,000 BTC.[^4]

| Field | Detail |
| --- | --- |
| Date of breach | 2 August 2016[^2] |
| Asset stolen | Bitcoin (BTC)[^2] |
| Exchange | Bitfinex, a virtual currency exchange founded in Hong Kong[^5] |
| Parent company | iFinex, according to secondary coverage[^6] |
| Amount stolen | 119,754 to 119,756 BTC, depending on source[^1][^2][^7] |
| Customer compensation | BFX tokens credited at 1 BFX per US dollar lost[^2] |
| Seizure | More than 94,000 BTC, valued at over $3.6 billion at the time of seizure[^4] |

## History

CoinDesk reported on 2 August 2016 that Bitfinex had taken its trading platform offline after a reported hack that resulted in the theft of customer funds.[^imp-41-1] Cointelegraph put the value of the stolen bitcoin at $72 million at the time.[^imp-41-2]

A TechCrunch headline on 2 August 2016 reported that bitcoin dropped 20% after about $70 million worth was stolen from Bitfinex.[^imp-41-3] The headline reports the sequence of the two events only. The sources reviewed do not establish a confirmed cause for the fall in price.

The US Department of Justice said the case was investigated by the IRS Criminal Investigation Washington, D.C. Field Office's Cyber Crimes Unit, the FBI's Chicago Field Office and HSI New York, with help from the Ansbach Police Department in Germany.[^4] On 8 February 2022, the DOJ announced the arrests in Manhattan of Ilya Lichtenstein and Heather Morgan, both of New York. A criminal complaint alleged that they conspired to launder the proceeds of the theft.[^4] Agents executed court-authorised search warrants on online accounts and obtained files containing the private keys to the wallet that directly received the stolen funds, which allowed them to seize more than 94,000 BTC.[^4] Deputy Attorney General Lisa O. Monaco described the seizure as the department's largest financial seizure ever.[^4] The DOJ later said the government had seized another approximately $475 million tied to the hack since the arrests.[^1]

According to court documents cited by the DOJ, Lichtenstein used advanced hacking tools to access Bitfinex's network, fraudulently authorised more than 2,000 transactions moving 119,754 BTC to a wallet he controlled, and then deleted access credentials and log files.[^1] The DOJ describes laundering methods including fictitious identities, automated transactions, deposits into darknet markets and exchanges, chain-hopping, mixing services such as Bitcoin Fog, Helix and ChipMixer, and gold coins that Morgan concealed by burying them.[^1] On 3 August 2023, Lichtenstein pleaded guilty to conspiracy to commit money laundering. Morgan pleaded guilty to one count of money laundering conspiracy and one count of conspiracy to defraud the United States.[^1] A DOJ press release, reproduced on a third-party site, says Lichtenstein was later sentenced to five years in prison.[^9] The sources reviewed do not establish the sentencing date or Morgan's sentence.

Sources differ slightly on the amount stolen. Bitfinex's FAQ gives 119,755 BTC,[^imp-72-2] while The Block, citing the government, gives 119,754 BTC.[^imp-72-3] The sources reviewed do not confirm the figure of 119,756 BTC that appears in the summary table.

In August 2020, Bitfinex published a statement pledging rewards for information leading to the recovery of the funds and for the return of the stolen property.[^16] On 7 October 2020, more than $26 million in bitcoin tied to the 2016 hack was moved across seven transactions, according to The Block, which relied on the Whale Alert data bot.[^16]

In January 2025, the US government filed a court document in the US District Court for the District of Columbia in a case involving Ilya Lichtenstein and Heather Morgan. The filing argued that more than 94,000 bitcoin should be returned to Bitfinex as in-kind restitution under the defendants' plea agreements, and that the restitution should include all of the assets seized from the Bitfinex Hack Wallet.[^imp-73-3] The government said there is no "victim" for the specific offenses of conviction in the proceeding, but that the court has the authority to order voluntary restitution pursuant to the defendants' plea agreements.[^imp-73-3] The filing is a request, and the sources reviewed do not establish whether the court has ruled on it.[^imp-73-3]

## Governance

BFX token holders could sell the tokens on the market or sell them to Bitfinex at a fixed rate of $1 per token.[^3] Users could also take iFinex shares in place of BFX tokens.[^6] The exchange's FAQ says that all BFX token holders had their tokens redeemed at 100 cents on the US dollar or exchanged for shares of the capital stock of iFinex Inc. within eight months of the breach, and that all BFX tokens were destroyed.[^2]

Bitfinex's FAQ says that the company created a tradable Recovery Right Token (RRT) for certain BFX holders that converted BFX tokens into shares of iFinex.[^imp-73-1] A Bitfinex blog post dated 11 October 2016 described the RRT as a token created to compensate victims of the security breach and to give priority to early BFX conversions, and said that no RRTs would be delivered for subscriptions made after 30 November 2016.[^imp-73-2] The FAQ says that any recovered funds will be used for distributions to RRT holders, up to $1 per RRT.[^imp-73-1] The FAQ also says that, if Bitfinex receives a recovery, it will within 18 months use 80% of the recovered net funds to repurchase and burn outstanding UNUS SED LEO tokens. This is a company statement.[^imp-73-1]

## Incidents and controversies

Mt. Gox, a bitcoin exchange, reported in February 2014 that about 850,000 bitcoin were missing and filed for bankruptcy protection.[^imp-41-4]

In the restitution dispute, Judge Colleen Kollar-Kotelly asked prosecutors to clarify, according to Cointelegraph. The judge noted that returning the funds to Bitfinex “would appear to have the effect of reducing the amount of the forfeiture order.”[^18]
